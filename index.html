Session 2 • Active Lesson

Simplifying Expressions → Ready to Solve

Main idea: simplifying is not a bag of tricks. It is the process of rewriting an expression in an equivalent, cleaner form. Today you will use structure, the distributive property, and like terms to do that accurately.

How we work: the screen coaches. The mathematics belongs on paper. For every new idea: explain → demonstrate → work by hand → discuss reasoning → check → increase difficulty.

1. Learning Objectives

By the end of this session, Shivam will be able to:

  • identify which terms are like terms and explain why they can or cannot be combined;
  • simplify expressions accurately, including positive and negative coefficients;
  • use the distributive property in written form and connect it to an area model and box model;
  • simplify multi-step expressions by distributing first, then combining like terms, then writing standard form;
  • recognize that FOIL is only a compact way to organize repeated distribution for two binomials;
  • show enough mastery to decide whether the next step should be more expression practice or the beginning of equation solving.
Structure→Distribute→Combine→Standard form→Check

2. Warm-up Retrieval

Do all five on paper. Do not enter answers on the website. After each, explain the rule you used.

  1. 18 ÷ 3 + 2(4)
  2. 5 + 3² - 2
  3. 7x + 4x - 3
  4. 4y - (-3y) + 2
  5. Write 8 + 2x + 5x² - x in standard form.
Tutor check: Look for correct order of operations, attention to signs, and whether Shivam can explain why x and x² are unlike terms.

3. Like Terms

Explain

Terms are like terms when their variable parts match exactly. The coefficients may be different, but the variable and exponent must match.

3x + 8x = 11x

4x² - 7x² = -3x²

But x, x², and constants are three different kinds of terms.

Demonstrate

6x + 4 - 2x + 9

Group mentally by type: (6x - 2x) + (4 + 9)

4x + 13

Ask: “What feature tells you two terms belong together?” Do not accept “because they both have letters” as a complete explanation.

Student works by hand

  1. 5x + 7x + 3
  2. 9y - 4y + 8 - 2
  3. 3a² + 5a - a² + 2a
  4. -6m + 2 + 9m - 7
Discuss reasoning: For #3, Shivam should explicitly separate the a² terms from the a terms. Then increase difficulty by asking him to rewrite the result in standard form without prompting.

4. Distributive Property Workshop

The distributive property means one factor multiplies every term inside a grouped expression. The different models below show the same mathematics from different angles.

A. Area Model

Think of 3(x + 4) as a rectangle with height 3 and total width x + 4. Split the width into x and 4. The two smaller areas are 3x and 12.

3(x + 4) = 3x + 12

Area Model Visualizer

Enter a number outside the parentheses and two inside terms. Use forms such as 3, x, 4 or -2, 3y, -5.

3x
12

3(x + 4) = 3x + 12

After using the visual, close the loop on paper: draw one area model by hand for -2(3y - 5) and explain how the negative factor affects both regions.

B. Standard Written Distribution

Write the multiplication twice so the factor cannot “miss” a term.

-4(2x - 3)

=(-4)(2x) + (-4)(-3)

= -8x + 12

Language cue: “Multiply the outside factor by every term inside. Keep the sign attached to the term.”

C. Box Method

The box method is an organizer for repeated distribution. For two binomials, every term in one factor must multiply every term in the other factor.

(x + 3)(x + 5)

x²
5x
3x
15

Then combine the like terms: x² + 8x + 15.

Box Method Builder

This organizer is for seeing all four products. Type a simple binomial in each factor using coefficients of 1, such as x + 2 and x - 4.

5x
3x
15

Four products: x², 5x, 3x, 15. Combine like terms on paper.

D. Where FOIL Fits

FOIL is not a different rule. It is simply a memory label for the four distributions that occur when multiplying two binomials: First, Outer, Inner, Last.

(x + 3)(x + 5)

x·x + x·5 + 3·x + 3·5

x² + 5x + 3x + 15 = x² + 8x + 15

Important: Use FOIL only as a comparison today. The transferable idea is distribution: every term in one factor must multiply every term in the other factor. That idea still works when expressions have more than two terms.

5. Guided Practice

Work these on paper in order. The difficulty rises deliberately. For the early items, say each move aloud. As accuracy improves, reduce the verbal scaffolding.

  6. 6p - 2p² + 9 + 5p² - p
  7. 4(x + 6)
  8. 3(2y - 5)
  9. -2(x + 7)
  10. -5(3a - 4)
  11. 2(x + 3) + 5x
  12. 4(2y - 1) - 3y

  16. -2(4y - 3) + 5(y + 2)

Check and increase difficulty: After every 4–5 problems, stop for a 30-second explanation: “What did you look for first?” “Where could a sign error happen?” “How do you know the final expression is simplified?” If reasoning is sound, move immediately to the next block rather than over-practicing easy items.

6. Independent Practice

Five challenge problems. Paper only. Shivam should choose his own organization and show enough work that another person could audit each step.

  1. 6(2x - 3) - 4(x + 5) + 7
  2. -3(4y - 2) + 2(5y + 7) - 9
  3. Write 5 - 2x + 3x² - 4 + 7x - x² in standard form.
  4. 2(3a - 4) - [a + 2(a - 5)]
  5. 4(2m - 3) - 3(m + 1) + 2(1 - m)
Mastery evidence: Aim for at least 4 of 5 correct with clean distribution and no repeated sign/like-term error. Reasoning matters: a corrected arithmetic slip is different from a structural misunderstanding.

7. Decision Point

Use the independent set plus Shivam’s explanations. Choose one branch based on evidence, not on how much time remains.

Extension A

Extended Practice

Choose this if: fewer than 4/5 independent problems are correct, or distribution/sign errors repeat, or Shivam cannot explain why terms combine.

  1. 3(2x + 5) - x
  2. -4(y - 3) + 2y
  3. 5(2a - 1) - 3(a + 4)
  4. -2(3m + 5) + 4(m - 1)
  5. 2x² + 5x - x² - 8x + 6
  6. 3(2p - 4) - 2(p + 7) + 5

Diagnose the exact failure point. Re-model only that step, then return control to Shivam. Avoid restarting the whole lesson.

Extension B

Beginning Equation Solving

Choose this if: at least 4/5 are correct, errors are isolated, and Shivam can describe the simplification process without prompts.

Transition with one key distinction: an expression is simplified; an equation has an equals sign and is solved for values that make both sides equal.

Demonstrate

3x + 5 = 20

Undo +5 on both sides: 3x = 15. Then divide both sides by 3: x = 5.

Student works by hand

  1. x + 7 = 19
  2. 4x = 28
  3. 2x + 3 = 17
  4. 5y - 6 = 24
  5. 3(x + 2) = 18
  6. 2(x + 4) + x = 17

For the last two, ask: “Would simplifying first make the equation easier to solve?” This creates the bridge into the next lesson naturally.

8. Exit Ticket

Complete on paper without help. Then explain one answer aloud.

  1. -3(2x - 4) + 5x
  2. Write 4 + 2x² - 7x + 3x² + 2x - 1 in standard form.
  3. In one sentence: Why is FOIL an example of the distributive property rather than a separate kind of mathematics?
9. Hidden Tutor Notes

Instructional priorities

  • Move quickly. Shivam learned rapidly in Session 1; do not require unnecessary repetitions after clear mastery.
  • Protect handwritten reasoning. The three website tools are visual supports, not substitutes for paper work.
  • Watch negative distribution closely. A repeated error such as -3(x - 4) = -3x - 12 is conceptual, not just careless.
  • Use “same variable part” language for like terms. Require exponents to match.
  • When standard form appears, expect descending powers: x², then x, then constants.
