Strong algebra is built by seeing structure, writing clean steps, and explaining why each step works.

Session 2 • Active Lesson

Simplifying Expressions → Ready to Solve

Main idea: simplifying means rewriting an expression in an equivalent, cleaner form. Today the goal is not to memorize more tricks. It is to see structure, make legal moves, and explain why the rewritten expression still means the same thing.

How we work: the screen coaches. The mathematics belongs on paper. For every new idea: explain → demonstrate → work by hand → discuss reasoning → check → increase difficulty.

1. Learning Objectives

By the end of this session, Shivam will be able to:

  • identify like terms and explain why they can or cannot be combined;
  • simplify expressions accurately with positive and negative coefficients;
  • use the distributive property in written form and connect it to an area model and box model;
  • simplify multi-step expressions by distributing, combining like terms, and writing standard form;
  • recognize FOIL as repeated distribution for two binomials, not a separate mathematical rule;
  • distinguish simplifying an expression from solving an equation;
  • show enough mastery to decide whether to extend expression work or begin equation solving.
See structure→Distribute→Combine→Standard form→Check equivalence

3. Like Terms: What Can Actually Combine?

Explain

Terms are like terms when their variable parts match exactly. The coefficients may be different, but the variables and exponents must match.

3x + 8x = 11x

4x² - 7x² = -3x²

Think of the variable part as the “unit.” You may add 3 x-units to 8 x-units. But an x-unit and an x²-unit are different kinds of quantities.

Demonstrate

6x + 4 - 2x + 9

Group by type: (6x - 2x) + (4 + 9)

4x + 13

Discussion prompt: “What feature tells you two terms belong together?” Require “same variable part, including exponent,” not merely “they both have letters.”

Transition: “So far, every term we wanted to combine was already visible. But algebra often hides terms inside parentheses. Before we can combine those terms, we need a legal way to bring them out. That is exactly what the distributive property does.”

4. Distributive Property Workshop

The distributive property says that a factor outside a group multiplies every term inside that group. The models below are not different rules. They are different ways to see the same multiplication.

A. Area Model

Think of 3(x + 4) as a rectangle with height 3 and total width x + 4. Split the width into x and 4. The whole area equals the sum of the two smaller areas.

3(x + 4) = 3x + 12

Area Model Visualizer

Enter a numerical outside factor and two simple inside terms. Use forms such as 3, x, 4 or -2, 3y, -5.

3x
12

3(x + 4) = 3x + 12

Tutor move: Return immediately to paper. Draw an area model for -2(3y - 5). Ask, “What must happen to both regions when the outside factor is negative?” Expected result: -6y + 10.

B. Standard Written Distribution

When the picture is no longer needed, show both multiplications in writing so the outside factor cannot “miss” a term.

-4(2x - 3)

=(-4)(2x) + (-4)(-3)

= -8x + 12

Language cue: “Multiply the outside factor by every term inside. Keep each sign attached to its term.” Ask why the constant becomes positive 12.

D. Where FOIL Fits

FOIL is not a new rule. It is a memory label for the four products created when two binomials are multiplied: First, Outer, Inner, Last.

(x + 3)(x + 5)

x·x + x·5 + 3·x + 3·5

x² + 5x + 3x + 15 = x² + 8x + 15

Why this matters: FOIL works only because distribution works. Distribution is the transferable idea. It still works with three terms, more variables, negative terms, and expressions that do not fit the letters F-O-I-L.
Transition: “Distribution creates the terms. Combining like terms cleans them up. Most multi-step simplification is those two ideas working together in the correct order.”

5. Multi-Step Simplification: A Reliable Process

When an expression becomes longer, do not hunt randomly for things to combine. Use a consistent sequence:

1. Read the structure→2. Distribute→3. Combine like terms→4. Standard form→5. Quick check

Teacher model

3(2x - 5) + 2(x + 4)

Distribute: 6x - 15 + 2x + 8

Combine: (6x + 2x) + (-15 + 8)

Simplified: 8x - 7

Discussion prompts: “Why did we distribute before combining?” “Where was the highest-risk sign?” “How can you tell there is nothing left to simplify?”

6. Misconception Clinic

This section is diagnostic. Do not lecture through every box. Show the incorrect work, ask Shivam to locate the first illegal move, and use only the misconception that matches what you are seeing.

Missed distribution

3(x + 4) = 3x + 4

Ask: “Which term never got multiplied by 3?”

Repair: Draw two arrows or two area regions. The factor 3 must reach both x and 4.

Negative sign error

-3(x - 4) = -3x - 12

Ask: “What is (-3)(-4)?”

Repair: Treat -4 as one signed term. Negative times negative gives positive.

Unlike terms combined

2x² + 5x = 7x²

Ask: “Do the variable parts match exactly?”

Repair: x² and x are different term families, so the expression is already simplified.

Exponent changed while adding

3x + 4x = 7x²

Ask: “Are we multiplying x by x, or counting x-terms?”

Repair: Adding coefficients does not change the variable part: 3x + 4x = 7x.

Trying to solve an expression

4x + 7 → x = ?

Ask: “What number is this expression equal to?”

Repair: Without an equals sign there is no condition that determines x. We simplify expressions; we solve equations.

Stopping too early

2(x + 3) + 5x = 2x + 6 + 5x

Ask: “Are any like terms still visible?”

Repair: Continue to 7x + 6.

7. Independent Mastery Check

Paper only. No coaching unless Shivam is genuinely stuck. Show enough work that another person could audit each step.

  1. 6(2x - 3) - 4(x + 5) + 7
  2. -3(4y - 2) + 2(5y + 7) - 9
  3. Write 5 - 2x + 3x² - 4 + 7x - x² in standard form.
  4. 2(3a - 4) - [a + 2(a - 5)]
  5. 4(2m - 3) - 3(m + 1) + 2(1 - m)
Mastery evidence: Move to equation solving if at least 4 of 5 are correct, errors are isolated rather than repeated, and Shivam can explain the order of his moves. A quickly self-corrected arithmetic slip is different from a repeated structural error.

  2. -4(y - 3) + 2y

Tutor move: Identify the first wrong move, model one parallel example, then return control to Shivam. Do not restart the entire lesson.

Branch B

Bridge Into Equation Solving

Choose this if: at least 4/5 mastery problems are correct and Shivam can describe the simplification process without prompts.

Expression

2(x + 4) + x

Goal: rewrite it more simply.

2x + 8 + x = 3x + 8

There is no single value of x to find.

Equation

2(x + 4) + x = 17

Goal: find the value of x that makes both sides equal.

First simplify the left side, then solve.

Teacher transition: “Everything you just learned still matters. Equations add one new responsibility: once each side is as clear as it needs to be, we use inverse operations while keeping the equation balanced.”

Balance idea

An equation says two quantities are equal. Whatever operation we perform to one side must also be performed to the other side, so the equality remains true.

3x + 5 = 20

Subtract 5 from both sides: 3x = 15.

Divide both sides by 3: x = 5.

Check: 3(5)+5=20, so the solution works.

Student works by hand

  1. x + 7 = 19
  2. 4x = 28
  3. 2x + 3 = 17
  4. 5y - 6 = 24
  5. 3(x + 2) = 18
  6. 2(x + 4) + x = 17
  7. 4(x - 1) + 2x = 20
  8. 3(2y + 1) - y = 18

Key prompt for #5–8: “What can you simplify before you start undoing operations?” If Shivam independently simplifies first, that is strong evidence for Session 3.

10. Hidden Tutor Notes & Teacher Edition

Instructional priorities

  • Pace: Shivam learned quickly in Session 1. Once a skill is accurate and explainable, advance rather than assigning repetition for its own sake.
  • Paper first: The website tools make structure visible. They do not replace handwritten algebra.
  • Language: Use “same variable part, including exponent” for like terms and “keep the sign attached to the term” for distribution.
  • Negative distribution: A repeated error such as -3(x - 4) = -3x - 12 is conceptual. Rebuild the signed multiplication, not merely the arithmetic answer.
  • Standard form: Expect descending powers: x², then x, then constants.
  • Equation bridge: Do not say “move it to the other side.” Name the inverse operation and show it on both sides so balance is preserved.

Suggested transitions

Like terms → distribution: “We know how to combine terms once we can see them. Parentheses can hide those terms. Distribution brings them into view without changing the value of the expression.”

Distribution → multi-step simplification: “Distribution creates the terms; combining like terms cleans them up.”

Expressions → equations: “Simplifying changes the form but keeps the same value for every x. Solving asks which x makes one equality true.”

Diagnostic decision rules

  • One arithmetic slip, corrected independently: continue.
  • Repeated missed distribution: return to arrows/area model for one example.
  • Repeated negative-sign error: isolate signed multiplication before returning to the full expression.
  • Combining unlike terms: sort by variable part/exponent before doing arithmetic.
  • Correct answers but weak explanation: ask for justification before advancing to equations.
